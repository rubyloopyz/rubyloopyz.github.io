How many online orders will each day of Q4 bring, through 10.10, 11.11 and 12.12?

79% of pre-picked units sold on the 8.8 campaign day itself, in a backtest on unseen data Beat the previous rule at the same fill rate

Decathlon Thailand · Aug 2026

How many parcels will a double-date sale bring, and what can be picked before it starts?

A daily parcel forecast built from packing and pick-line exports: a monthly baseline, day-of-week factors and a campaign multiplier, converted into a headcount plan. On top of it sits an article-level pre-pick list, so the warehouse clears part of the peak before orders land.

The finding that shaped the design: campaign demand is not concentrated. The top 20 articles are only about a tenth of a peak day's units, so a short list can't do the job and the rule has to score thousands of articles.

How it was checked

Six candidate rules, each trained on June and July and scored on 8.8. The chosen one sold 79% of its units on the day, and 4.6% were still unsold five days later.

5.4% of free-text return comments give an actual customer reason First pass said 8.6%. Recounted before reporting

Decathlon Thailand · Sept 2026

Why do customers return things? The return log mostly can't say.

Asked for the causes behind returns, I checked whether the data could name them before counting anything. Every free-text comment across five return and case logs, most of them in Thai, was translated and judged one by one.

Most comments are shipping status and internal notes. The field that is complete tells a clearer story: 95.5% of returned lines go straight back to stock, so defects are a small share, and the top two stated reasons are fulfilment errors (missing items, wrong items).

How it was checked

A manual audit found the keyword pass was counting delivery failures as customer reasons. Thai has no spaces between words, so short keywords matched inside unrelated ones and four had to be removed. The recommendation was a required reason at registration, and since then the field is being filled in: about 1 in 14 returns by October, up from 1 in 39 in August.

0.840 ROC-AUC on held-out customers, against 0.500 for a do-nothing baseline Beats baseline on a held-out split

Personal · IBM Telco dataset, 7,032 accounts

Which customers leave, what does it cost, and can we see it coming?

Churn costs $139,130 a month, 30.5% of recurring revenue. Month-to-month customers churn at 42.7% against 2.8% on two-year contracts, and hold 87% of the revenue at risk.

Churned customers appear to pay more, which points straight at a price cut. Split by internet tier, the gap reverses in all three tiers: monthly charge stands in for which product was bought, so it's no case for cutting prices.

How it was checked

Stratified hold-out, weighted toward recall because a missed churner costs more than a wasted offer. The reversal was re-tested under contract-type stratification, where it doesn't hold, so the claim is scoped to what survived.

46.7% of email tickets met the first-response target, against 100% for chat Silent timestamp bug found and fixed

Personal · Simulated IT service desk, Sept 2026

Where does an IT service desk miss its response targets?

First response is met 78.7% of the time and resolution 67.6%, counted in business hours only. Tickets raised on Monday before 11:00 meet the response target 66.5% of the time, against 80.6% otherwise.

The category mix is about the same on every channel, so the email gap isn't harder problems arriving by email.

How it was checked

A ticket landing a split second before 18:00 stalled the business-hours loop, and its safety cap returned a wrong time without an error for 141 tickets. I fixed it by jumping straight to closing time, and SLA flags now separate met, breached and still running.

Personal · 2,063 hourly observations, 10 assets

Does market sentiment move crypto prices? Not in this data.

Eight candidate signals tested against 24-hour returns; none reach significance. The sentiment measures correlate with each other (up to 0.66) and not with price. The column labelled prediction confidence tracks news volume, not accuracy.

How it was checked

Chronological train/test split, a mean-prediction baseline and lagged daily tests. The distributions turned out to be synthetic, uniform where real sentiment has fat tails, so the write-up says the null result belongs to the file rather than to markets.
